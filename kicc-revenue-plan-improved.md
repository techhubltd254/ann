## 2. Revenue Position, Years 1–5

The ramp curve for each pipeline is: Year 1 = 15% of steady-state, Year 2 = 45%, Year 3 = 75%, Year 4 = 95%, Year 5 = 100%. The curve is the same for all 87 pipelines. Variation in category performance comes from differences in steady-state value, not launch timing.

Revenue compounds as the ramp matures. The progression is:

- **Year 1** (896,175,000): All 87 pipelines at 15% adoption. Early-stage onboarding focused on high-value categories — marketplace, tourism, real estate — that have the shortest time-to-revenue per seller.
- **Year 2** (2,688,525,000): Ramp reaches 45%. Agriculture pipelines (coffee, horticulture, maize) begin contributing material volume. Rental network and financial services lines gain traction.
- **Year 3** (4,480,875,000): Ramp reaches 75%. Minerals (gold, gemstones, limestone) and logistics move through the ramp inflection point.
- **Year 4** (5,675,775,000): Ramp reaches 95%. Energy, escrow float, flagship venues and bulk trade approach maturity.
- **Year 5** (5,974,500,000): Full 100% ramp across all 87 pipelines. No pipeline remains in a ramp period.

## 7. Channel Strategy by Sector

[Same as before — no change]

The screentime-to-airtime split differs by category. Each split follows where the sector's buyers are reached.

**Agriculture — 70% airtime.** Smallholder farmers are reached primarily through vernacular and county radio. Screentime is reserved for the export-facing segments (cut flowers, avocado, macadamia) where buyers are corporate.

**Government — 65% airtime.** County service announcements and licensing messages travel best over radio and SMS; television is used for national campaigns only.

**Tourism — 75% screentime.** Tourism buyers research visually before booking. Television, video platforms, and digital out-of-home carry the booking volume.

**Real Estate and Rental — 70% screentime.** Property decisions are visual and location-specific. Static out-of-home near target neighbourhoods and digital display dominate.

**Minerals and Industrial — 60% airtime / 40% screentime.** Investor audiences are reached through business press, digital video, and targeted radio, not mass television.

**Commerce — 65% screentime.** Digital display and video drive B2B marketplace sign-ups; radio covers the county-pavilion and farm-trade segments.

**Financial Services — 60% screentime.** Financial products need credible visual formats; SMS and mobile channels carry the transactional reminders.

**Logistics and Additional Sectors — 50/50.** No channel dominates; budget follows campaign-by-campaign test results.

## 8. The Target Gap and the Improvement Programme

The USD 12,000,000 monthly steady-state target equals **KES 1,548,000,000 per month**. Year 5 nets to 468,002,500 per month. The shortfall of 1,079,997,500 per month is structural; all 87 pipelines are already at full ramp. Closing the gap depends on lifting the scale factor or expanding the addressable market.

### 8.1 Onboarding speed programme

All 87 pipelines are available from Year 1. The ramp curve (15%, 45%, 75%, 95%, 100%) is determined by the speed at which sellers, buyers and investors are onboarded onto each pipeline.

Measures to raise boarding speed:
- Pre-onboard the top 20 county governments with committed pavilion trade and collection volumes before Year 1 launch, lifting the Year 1 ramp from 15% to 20% and Year 2 ramp from 45% to 55%. Estimated cumulative benefit Years 1–4: **KES 180,000,000 – 240,000,000**.
- Deploy a single-credential onboarding flow across all 87 pipelines so that a verified seller on any pipeline is pre-approved for every other pipeline, reducing friction.
- Incentivise cross-pipeline referrals through the Trust Score algorithm (higher weight for multi-pipeline activity).

### 8.2 Pricing discipline on the four anchor categories

Holding the marketplace commission floor at 2.5% rather than 2.0%, and pricing the tourism merchant-of-record bundle at a 25% blended commission rather than 20%, adds an estimated **KES 150,000,000 – 200,000,000 to Year 5 revenue** without requiring new buyers.

### 8.3 Regional roll-out into the East African corridor

Extending the marketplace and rental networks into Tanzania, Uganda, and Rwanda along the Mombasa–Nairobi–Lake Victoria corridor raises the scale factor from 1.5 to 2.0 by Year 5. At a 2.0 factor, Year 5 gross revenue reaches approximately **KES 7,966,000,000**, and net run-rate rises from 468,002,500 to approximately **KES 625,000,000 per month**. The corridor expansion is the single highest-lift measure in the programme.

### 8.4 Capital-market lines at scale

Direct lending is the largest single lever on revenue capacity once licensed. A lending book of KES 2,000,000,000 at a 6% net margin contributes KES 120,000,000 per year, compounding as the book grows. This line is modeled as an option pending CBK approval, not as committed revenue.

### 8.5 Effect of the programme

| Scenario | Year 5 gross (KES/yr) |
|---|---:|
| Base model (all-Y1 launch, scale 1.5) | 5,974,500,000 |
| Base + onboarding incentives (8.1) + pricing (8.2) | 6,400,000,000 – 6,700,000,000 |
| With regional roll-out at 2.0 scale factor | 7,966,000,000 |
| With regional + capital markets | 8,500,000,000 – 9,000,000,000 |

Even with all measures combined, the USD 12,000,000 monthly target (KES 18,576,000,000 per year) is not reached within five years at the current steady-state estimates. The target is feasible with the regional corridor and licensed lending succeeding in combination, plus a scale factor of 2.5 or higher beyond Year 5.

## 10. Risk Register

1. **Onboarding ramp.** All 87 pipelines launch Year 1, so the entire model rests on the speed of seller and buyer onboarding across categories simultaneously. A slower ramp compounds across all pipelines. Mitigation: quarterly reforecast against signed pipeline volume, not expected volume.
2. **Regulatory timing.** Direct lending, equity facilitation, and escrow float require CBK action and are outside the Corporation's control. Mitigation: carry as options; do not fund operations against them.
3. **Concentration.** Four categories carry 75% of Year 5 revenue. Mitigation: assign a named owner for each category; report category-level actuals monthly.
4. **Estimate quality.** Steady-state values derive from the catalog's scale language, not sourced sector data. Mitigation: commission KNBS, CBK, and sector-board validation before fundraising use.
5. **Advertising efficiency.** The marketing ratio (14% down to 6%) assumes acquisition cost declines as the brand compounds. If cost-per-acquisition does not fall, either spend rises or ramp slows. Mitigation: track cost per verified seller and cost per transaction monthly.
