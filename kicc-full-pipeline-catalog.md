# KICC Full Pipeline Catalog
### Every income-generating line, described individually — not grouped into categories that hide the count
### 87 pipelines — Section M added: flagship venues, universal site ticketing, equity/investment, the multi-zone SEZ marketplace solution, and bulk trade brokerage. Pipeline 79 corrected to its true scope: a universal, Core-level rating engine, not a real-estate-only feature.

Each entry: what it is, how KICC makes money from it specifically, and what it needs before it can run. Commodities that were previously lumped as "Agriculture" or "Minerals" are split into one pipeline per product, as requested — each has a different buyer, a different price cycle, and a different aggregation problem, so they are not interchangeable.

## A. Agriculture — one pipeline per product

**1. Coffee**
KICC aggregates smallholder lots into export-grade consignments, verifies grading (AA/AB/PB etc.) at collection, and matches to international roasters/importers. Kenya's coffee auction system (Nairobi Coffee Exchange) already exists — KICC's role is upstream aggregation and downstream market access for growers outside the cooperative-society system, plus traceability data for EU due-diligence requirements. Revenue: 3–5% commission on export value, plus a cut of any pre-shipment financing advanced against a confirmed contract.

**2. Tea**
Similar aggregation model, but Kenya's tea sector already runs through the Mombasa Tea Auction and KTDA for smallholders — KICC's opening is independent/non-KTDA smallholders and specialty/orthodox tea (higher margin, less institutionally served) rather than competing with the auction itself. Revenue: 3–5% commission on matched export volume for the segment not already served.

**3. Maize**
Domestic staple, not export-led — the opportunity is county-to-county and surplus-to-deficit-region distribution (Rift Valley surplus to deficit counties), reducing post-harvest loss and price volatility. Revenue: commission on matched domestic trade volume (1–2%, thinner margin, but very high volume) plus a storage/warehousing referral fee (see Pipeline 44).

**4. Sugarcane**
Contracted to milling factories already, so KICC's role is different: side-selling prevention and price-transparency data (farmers currently underpaid due to opaque weighing/grading at factory gates), sold as a verification service to millers and out-grower associations. Revenue: subscription/per-tonne verification fee, not a trade commission.

**5. Horticulture — cut flowers**
Kenya's largest single export earner in this category. KICC's role: aggregate smallholder and mid-size growers (outside the large Lake Naivasha estates, which already have direct export relationships) and match to European/Middle Eastern buyers, with cold-chain and airfreight booking bundled in. Revenue: 3–5% export commission + logistics-booking referral fee.

**6. Horticulture — avocado**
Fast-growing export category with a large, fragmented smallholder base and real EU market-access requirements (GlobalG.A.P. certification). KICC's role: pool smallholder volume to meet minimum export container sizes, manage certification tracking. Revenue: 3–5% export commission.

**7. Horticulture — macadamia**
Similar smallholder-fragmentation profile to avocado, high per-kg value, strong Asian buyer demand. Revenue: 3–5% export commission plus a grading/quality-verification fee (macadamia pricing is highly grade-sensitive).

**8. Horticulture — French beans, snow peas, other vegetables**
High-frequency, lower per-unit-value export horticulture, mostly air-freighted to UK/EU supermarkets. KICC's role: smallholder aggregation and supermarket-standard compliance tracking (pesticide residue, packaging). Revenue: 3–5% export commission.

**9. Livestock & dairy**
Domestic-market-focused: cattle/goat marketing (reducing distress sales at farm-gate prices) and dairy cooperative bypass for smallholders not served by existing cooperatives. Revenue: commission on matched livestock sales (1–3%) and a milk-collection-verification fee for dairy (paid by processors wanting accurate volume/quality data).

**10. Poultry**
Domestic broiler and egg trade, fragmented small-scale producers supplying urban markets. Revenue: marketplace commission on matched B2B trade (small producer to hotel/restaurant/retailer), 2–4%.

**11. Cotton**
A revival-stage sector (Kenya's cotton output has declined from historical highs) — KICC's role is linking any remaining/returning cotton growers to the textile/apparel manufacturing pipeline (Pipeline 22), creating a domestic supply chain rather than relying on imported cotton for SEZ garment factories. Revenue: commission on matched domestic supply contracts; strategically valuable even at low initial volume because it feeds Pipeline 22.

**12. Fisheries & blue economy**
Lake Victoria, coastal, and aquaculture fish trade — currently heavily informal with significant post-catch loss. KICC's role: cold-chain-linked aggregation from landing sites to urban/export markets. Revenue: 2–4% commission on matched trade, plus cold-chain logistics referral.

**13. Aquaculture (fish farming)**
Distinct from wild-catch fisheries — a growing, government-promoted sector with fragmented small producers needing market access and feed-input financing. Revenue: marketplace commission plus an input-financing referral (feed, fingerlings) similar to the agri input-financing model.

**14. Forestry & timber**
Licensed/verified timber trade — a sector with real illegal-logging and chain-of-custody problems that a verified-provenance platform can address. Revenue: transaction commission (2–4%) plus a chain-of-custody/provenance-verification fee sellable to buyers requiring legal-sourcing proof (construction firms, exporters).

**15. Edible oils (sunflower, other oilseeds)**
Import-substitution opportunity — Kenya imports a large share of edible oil; domestic oilseed aggregation feeding local processors reduces that. Revenue: commission on matched grower-to-processor supply contracts.

## B. Mineral & Natural Resources — one pipeline per resource

**16. Soda ash**
Lake Magadi is a globally significant source; already has an established operator (Tata Chemicals Magadi) — KICC's opening here is data/investment-matchmaking for expansion capacity or adjacent mineral rights, not aggregation of an already-consolidated resource. Revenue: facilitation fee on any matched investment or offtake introduction, same mechanism as Industrial/SEZ matchmaking (Pipeline 23).

**17. Titanium / mineral sands**
Kwale County deposits, currently operated by an established mining company — same logic as soda ash: KICC's role is investor/offtake matchmaking for adjacent blocks or expansion, plus county-level data (the classification algorithm) showing investors what's near the resource (labour, power, port access). Revenue: matchmaking success fee (0.1–0.5% of matched investment value).

**18. Gold**
Western Kenya artisanal and small-scale gold mining (ASM) is large, fragmented, and largely informal/undocumented — a real formalization opportunity. KICC's role: verified-provenance aggregation from licensed ASM operators to legitimate buyers, addressing both smuggling losses to the state and price exploitation of miners. Revenue: 3–5% commission on verified trade volume — meaningfully higher-margin than most agri lines because informal-market price exploitation is currently severe.

**19. Gemstones (ruby, tsavorite, other coloured stones)**
Kenya is a known source of tsavorite and other coloured gemstones, mined at small scale and often undervalued by miners due to lack of grading expertise and market access. KICC's role: grading/certification partnership plus matching to international jewellery-trade buyers. Revenue: commission (5–10%, higher than bulk commodities because per-unit value is high and current miner price-realization is very low) plus a certification fee.

**20. Limestone, sand & quarry aggregates**
Construction-input minerals — high domestic volume, low per-unit value, heavily regional (transport cost dominates, so this is a hyper-local matching problem: nearest quarry to nearest construction site). Revenue: thin commission (1–2%) but very high transaction frequency, tied into the Real Estate/construction pipelines as a natural input-supply add-on.

**21. Fluorspar, coal & other exploration-stage minerals (Kitui coal, etc.)**
Early-stage or underdeveloped deposits. KICC's role here is pure data and investor-matchmaking — the same Industrial/SEZ mechanism, sold as "which mineral resources exist in which county, with what infrastructure nearby" to prospective investors, mining licence holders, or joint-venture partners. Revenue: data/matchmaking fee only; no operational trade to commission on until a resource is actively being mined at scale.

## C. Industrial, Manufacturing & SEZ-linked pipelines

**22. Textiles & apparel manufacturing investment**
Matchmaking investors into SEZ-designated garment/textile manufacturing capacity (AGOA-linked export apparel is a real, current Kenyan industry). Revenue: 0.1–0.5% success fee on matched investment, plus workforce-matching fees once factories are operating (Pipeline 26).

**23. General Industrial & SEZ investment matchmaking**
The parent pipeline for any manufacturing investor not covered by a named sub-sector above — fertilizer, cement, agro-processing plants, electronics/automotive assembly. Revenue: 0.1–0.5% success fee on committed investment.

**24. Leather & footwear manufacturing**
Kenya has a real domestic leather resource (from the livestock pipeline, #9) currently mostly exported raw/semi-processed rather than turned into finished goods locally — an investment-matchmaking opportunity tying Pipeline 9's supply to manufacturing capacity. Revenue: success fee on matched investment, plus ongoing input-supply commission linking hides/skins producers to tanneries/factories.

**25. Compliance-as-a-service (SEZ enterprises)**
A recurring subscription product for SEZ-based manufacturers, handling KRA/customs record-keeping obligations under the 2026 Economic Zones Bill. Revenue: monthly/annual subscription per enterprise, independent of transaction volume.

**26. Workforce-matching-as-a-service**
Connects verified, skills-tagged individuals (an extension of the Identity/KYB registry) to SEZ and manufacturing employers. Revenue: per-successful-hire fee or an employer subscription to the labour pool.

## D. Energy & Infrastructure Investment

**27. Geothermal investment matchmaking**
Kenya is a globally significant geothermal producer (Olkaria) with room for further private investment in generation capacity. Revenue: matchmaking success fee, same mechanism as mineral/industrial matchmaking.

**28. Solar (utility-scale and off-grid/mini-grid)**
Both utility-scale solar investment matchmaking and, separately, a distribution/financing role for off-grid household and business solar in underserved counties — the latter ties directly into the Foundational/Anchor county classification, since off-grid solar is exactly the kind of infrastructure those counties need. Revenue: matchmaking fee for utility-scale; commission/financing-referral fee for off-grid distribution.

**29. Wind power investment matchmaking**
Same mechanism as geothermal/solar — Kenya has existing utility-scale wind capacity (Lake Turkana) and room for further sites. Revenue: matchmaking success fee.

**30. Water infrastructure & bulk water services**
Investment matchmaking for water infrastructure projects in Foundational/Anchor counties, plus (longer-term) a bulk-water-trading facilitation role between counties with surplus and deficit. Revenue: matchmaking fee initially; trade-facilitation commission once bulk water trading is operational — this is a slow-build line but directly serves the equity mandate.

## E. Real Estate & Property — split by segment

**31. Residential sale**
Titled-property sale with Ardhisasa verification and advocate-mediated conveyancing, as previously specced. Revenue: commission/success fee on sale value (1–3%, standard Kenyan real estate agency range).

**32. Residential rental**
Distinct from sale — shorter transaction cycle, recurring (renewal) revenue potential, lower verification bar than a title transfer (tenancy, not ownership, changes hands). Revenue: one-time placement fee (typically one month's rent equivalent) plus a smaller recurring management fee if KICC also handles rent collection/escrow.

**33. Commercial & office space**
Office leasing and sale — ties directly into the Discovery/Directory building-level model, since office buildings are exactly the inventory unit that layer captures. Revenue: leasing commission (higher per-transaction value than residential).

**34. Industrial & warehousing space**
Leasing of warehouse/industrial units — a distinct market from office/residential, serving the logistics and manufacturing pipelines directly (a business in the Cross-Border Logistics or Industrial/SEZ pipeline needs exactly this). Revenue: leasing commission, plus cross-sell value into Pipeline 44 (warehousing-as-a-service).

**35. Agricultural land banking & land trading**
Sale/lease of agricultural land, distinct from developed real estate — needs the same title-verification rigor as residential/commercial, but a different buyer profile (often a diaspora or institutional agricultural investor, linking to Pipeline 43). Revenue: commission on sale/lease value.

**36. Title insurance**
A real product gap given how central title-verification risk already is to the Real Estate pipeline. Revenue: 10–20% commission on premium, distributed the same way as Pipeline 46 (embedded insurance).

## F. Tourism & Hospitality — split by experience type

**37. Wildlife & safari tourism**
Maasai Mara, Amboseli, Tsavo — Kenya's highest-recognition tourism product globally. KICC's role: bundled-itinerary merchant-of-record model (flight + transfer + lodge + park fees + guide), as previously specced. Revenue: 20–30% blended commission across the bundle.

**38. Coastal & beach tourism**
Mombasa, Diani, Watamu — a distinct customer profile (leisure/beach vs. wildlife-safari) with different seasonality and different competing OTAs. Revenue: same bundled-merchant-of-record mechanism, packaged separately because it's sold to a different buyer intent.

**39. Cultural & heritage tourism**
Fort Jesus and similar heritage sites — lower per-visitor spend than safari/beach but high volume potential and directly supports the "un-accessed destinations" goal, since many cultural/heritage sites nationally are under-visited relative to the big-five circuit. Revenue: ticketing commission plus bundling into multi-stop itineraries with other categories.

**40. MICE (conference/exhibition) tourism**
Business travellers attending conferences/exhibitions hosted through the platform's own Commerce/exhibition pipeline (booths, county pavilions) — a natural cross-sell: someone attending a KICC-hosted exhibition also needs a hotel and transport. Revenue: booking commission on the accommodation/transport layer wrapped around an event the platform already knows is happening.

**41. Adventure & mountain tourism**
Mount Kenya climbing/trekking, whitewater, and similar — a smaller but growing niche market with specialized operators. Revenue: same bundled-commission mechanism, packaged for this specific buyer segment.

**42. Diaspora "come home" tourism**
Holiday-timed packages specifically marketed to diaspora Kenyans through SACCOs and church networks, sold in USD, higher-intent and higher-ticket than a cold international tourist. Revenue: same tourism commission mechanism, but with a distinct go-to-market and USD pricing/remote-KYC requirement.

**43. Diaspora investment gateway (Real Estate, SEZ, Agriculture land)**
Not tourism, but grouped near it because it shares the same buyer: a Kenyan abroad with dollars and no trusted remote channel. Applies remote KYC and USD-denominated transactions across Real Estate (31–35), Industrial/SEZ matchmaking (22–23), and agricultural land (35). Revenue: no new take rate — captures the same commissions already specified for those pipelines, from a currently-underserved buyer segment worth an estimated $1B/year in remittance money seeking a productive-investment channel.

## G. Commerce, Marketplace & Discovery

**44. General marketplace commission**
Domestic goods trade across the platform's core marketplace — the baseline escrow commission (2–5% of GMV) on any transaction not already covered by a product-specific pipeline above.

**45. Exhibitor subscriptions**
Flat monthly fee (KES 500/2,500/10,000 tiers) for marketplace presence, independent of transaction volume.

**46. Embedded insurance distribution**
A checkout-step plugin across every pipeline above with a natural insurance moment (goods-in-transit, crop/weather-index, travel, event liability, title). Revenue: 10–20% commission on premium.

**47. County-pavilion trade**
Marketplace trade specifically organized under a county's branded pavilion — same underlying commission mechanism as general marketplace commission, but with the revenue-attribution split to the county (per the onboarding-governance model) rather than being fully retained.

**48. Exhibition booths & physical event tickets**
Booking fee on physical exhibition space and visitor ticket sales for KICC-hosted events, with dynamic pricing bounded by min/max floors.

**49. Advertising & digital-screen inventory**
Featured placement, county co-marketing packages, referral/click-out fees, and physical digital-screen advertising — a yield-managed inventory-allocation engine, bounded below by the Trust Score fairness floor.

**50. Livestream viewer passes**
Single-event and all-access annual passes for livestreamed exhibitions/events, with real-time conversion-moment detection during a free/ad-supported stream.

**51. Building & property directory listings (Discovery layer)**
Free-tier building/tenant listings with paid visibility tiers on top — the foundational supply-side layer, monetized primarily through what it feeds (Pipelines 44, 52) rather than subscription revenue alone.

**52. Meeting room & venue booking**
Commission on previously-unaggregated meeting-room and event-space capacity in hotels, office blocks, church halls, and county social halls — the Discovery layer's first directly transactable, commission-earning inventory.

**53. National government portal contracts**
Not a commission-generating pipeline — a lead-scoring feature ranking ministry/agency engagement signals for the sales team, monetized indirectly through whatever contract the sales team closes, not through the platform's transaction mechanics.

## H. Logistics & Trade

**54. Cross-border e-commerce logistics**
International buyer (China, Russia, US, UK) ordering a Kenyan item — commission plus FX spread, with multi-carrier abstraction and customs/landed-cost calculation at checkout.

**55. Freight & warehousing-as-a-service**
Aggregating warehouse capacity (linking to Pipeline 34) and offering it as bookable storage/fulfilment infrastructure to marketplace sellers, particularly useful for the maize (Pipeline 3) and horticulture cold-chain pipelines. Revenue: booking/storage fee, separate from the real-estate leasing commission on the underlying space.

**56. Customs facilitation & documentation**
A service layer helping exporters (coffee, tea, horticulture, minerals) navigate customs declarations and certificates of origin — sellable as a per-shipment fee independent of the underlying trade commission.

**57. Cold-chain logistics for perishables**
A specialized transport/storage layer serving horticulture, fisheries, and dairy specifically — perishables have a much higher loss-to-delay sensitivity than any other cargo type, making this its own service line rather than a generic freight offering. Revenue: booking commission on cold-chain capacity.

## I. Financial Services & Capital

**58. Escrow commission (base rate)**
The 2–5% commission underlying most Commerce and product-specific trade pipelines above — listed separately here because it is a distinct mechanism (payment-hold-release) from the product-specific matching/aggregation work each pipeline also does.

**59. Escrow float / treasury yield**
Interest-spread margin on aggregate escrow balances awaiting release — pending confirmed CBK permission to retain interest on client funds.

**60. Credit-Data-as-a-Service**
Licensing anonymized, aggregated Trust Score and verified transaction history to banks/fintechs as an underwriting input — subscription or per-query fee, no lending licence required.

**61. Loan referral marketplace**
A second layer on top of Pipeline 60: referring platform businesses to partner lenders for a 1–3% finder's fee on originated loan value, once the data product has proven usage.

**62. Direct lending (licensed Financing pipeline)**
Origination and servicing of loans against confirmed exposure (procurement POs, agricultural export contracts) — requires CBK lending licence; the largest-ceiling financial-services line, gated entirely on regulatory approval.

**63. Input financing**
Advances against future harvest/delivery for agricultural inputs (seed, fertilizer, feed) recovered at point of sale — a distinct product from general lending because collateral is the future crop/livestock, not a fixed asset.

**64. KYB-as-a-service / verification API**
Reselling the platform's own business-verification infrastructure (KRA PIN validation, document checks, Trust Score) to third parties — banks, other platforms, insurers — who need to verify a Kenyan business but aren't otherwise KICC customers. Revenue: per-query or subscription API fee, built on infrastructure the platform needs anyway for its own onboarding.

## K. Additional sectors — brainstormed, not yet built into any prior document

**68. Education & skills certification marketplace**
Matching training providers (vocational, technical, university-adjacent) to learners, with a certification/credentialing layer that feeds directly into Pipeline 26 (workforce-matching for SEZ employers) — a verified credential is worth more to an employer than an unverified one. Revenue: referral fee from training providers, plus a certification-verification fee paid by employers checking a candidate's credential.

**69. Healthtech — facility directory & appointment booking**
Directory and booking layer for clinics and hospitals, starting with exactly the Foundational/Anchor county health facilities already prioritized in the county classification model — this gives that tier of counties a genuine service (not just subsidized listing) while building toward a booking-commission model in better-served counties. Revenue: booking/referral commission in commercially viable areas; free/subsidized listing in Foundational/Anchor counties as part of the equity mandate.

**70. Telemedicine referral**
A thin layer connecting patients to licensed telemedicine providers, particularly valuable in counties with low physician density. Revenue: referral fee per consultation booked, paid by the provider.

**71. Creative industries — event ticketing & rights marketplace**
Ticketing for concerts, film screenings, and cultural events beyond the exhibition/MICE pipeline already specced, plus a rights/licensing marketplace connecting Kenyan musicians and filmmakers to distributors. Revenue: ticketing commission plus a rights-deal facilitation fee.

**72. Sports facility booking & sports tourism**
Booking commission on stadium/pitch/court rental (an underused-capacity problem similar to meeting rooms), plus packaged sports-tourism itineraries (e.g., marathon tourism, given Kenya's global distance-running reputation) using the same bundled-itinerary mechanism as Tourism.

**73. ICT & BPO facilitation**
Kenya has an established and growing business-process-outsourcing sector; KICC's role is investor/site matchmaking (same mechanism as Industrial/SEZ) specifically for BPO and tech-enabled services investment, plus a verified-workforce pool (Pipeline 26's tech/digital-skills variant).

**74. Waste management & recycling marketplace**
Matching waste generators (municipal, industrial, commercial) to licensed recyclers/processors — an emerging, policy-supported sector in Kenya (extended producer responsibility regulations creating new compliance demand). Revenue: transaction commission plus a compliance-verification fee sellable to producers needing proof of proper waste handling.

**75. Professional services marketplace**
Verified architects, contractors, quantity surveyors, lawyers, and accountants — a natural adjacent to the Real Estate and Industrial/SEZ pipelines, where every transaction in those categories creates demand for exactly these professionals. Revenue: referral/lead-generation fee, or a subscription for professionals wanting visibility in the directory.

## L. Real Estate Rental Expansion & Hotel-Travel Experience Bundling

**76. Universal rental & lease placement network (all unit sizes, all towns)**
The core middleman model, made explicit: any landlord offering any unit — a bedsitter or one-bedroom in Kisumu, a two-bedroom in Eldoret, a four-bedroom in Nairobi — lists through KICC, and when a tenant signs a lease after being connected through the platform, KICC earns a placement commission. This generalizes Pipeline 32 (Residential rental) across geography and unit size rather than treating it as a Nairobi-centric or single-segment product — the value of the network is precisely that it works the same way in a secondary town as it does in the capital. Revenue: a placement fee at lease signing (typically a percentage of one month's or one year's rent, standard finder's-fee range), earned once per successful placement, with no cap on unit size or location.

**77. Airbnb & short-let placement — with intelligent experience cross-sell**
Short-stay/Airbnb-style bookings are a distinct segment from long-lease rental (Pipeline 76) because the booking itself is the trigger for a second sale: the moment someone books a short-let, the system has both their dates and their location, which is exactly the input the Itinerary & Trip Planning engine needs to offer a relevant paid experience — a national park visit, a cultural site, a day trip — timed to their actual stay. Revenue: booking commission on the short-let itself, plus the itinerary/experience commission already specified under the Tourism pipelines (37–43) on whatever the guest adds. The short-let booking becomes an entry point into the tourism revenue stack, not just a real estate transaction.

**78. Villas & mansionettes — premium rental commission**
High-end residential rental is treated as its own line because the buyer profile, price point, and service expectation are different from standard rental (Pipeline 76) — typically a corporate relocation, a diaspora returnee, or a high-net-worth tenant, all of whom justify a higher-touch verification and viewing process. Revenue: commission at a higher rate than standard rental placement, reflecting both the higher rent value and the additional verification/concierge work involved.

**79. Universal multi-agency rating engine — the mother system, applied across every listing type**
This is not a real-estate feature. It is a single Core-level rating engine — the same Sector-Adaptive Institutional Rating already specified for exhibitors and institutions — and every pipeline in this catalog that lists a person, business, property, venue, or site plugs into it rather than building its own scoring logic. The inputs are the same regardless of what's being rated: platform history (reviews, transaction/dispute record) plus verified cross-ministry government data — security agencies on safety record, the health ministry on sanitation/health-code compliance, the hospitality/tourism ministry on licensing and service standards, and any other ministry whose data is relevant to what's being sold (agriculture ministry data for a farm supplier, transport authority data for a logistics partner, mining ministry data for a mineral-resource operator). What changes per pipeline is only which subset of ministry data applies and how it's weighted — a hospital's rating draws differently on health-ministry data than a hotel's draws on tourism-ministry data, but both run through the same engine, the same fairness rule (purchased visibility can never outrank the earned score), and the same geographic indexing (a search surfaces the best-rated option in the area actually being searched). Concretely, this single engine is what powers: real estate building/landlord ratings (as detailed under Pipeline 76–78), hotel and venue ratings (Pipelines 37–43, 81, 83), exhibitor and institutional rankings on the core marketplace (the original Sector-Adaptive Institutional Rating use case), health-facility ratings (Pipeline 69), and logistics/transport-partner ratings (Pipelines 54–57). Revenue: no separate charge for the rating itself — it is the trust infrastructure that makes every paid-visibility line in this catalog (Pipeline 49 and its equivalents) sellable at all, and building it once here instead of once per pipeline is what keeps ratings consistent and keeps the fairness rule enforceable in one place rather than eight.

**80. Commercial space connection facilitation (shops, venues, conference space — any building)**
Generalizes the meeting-room booking model (Pipeline 52) and commercial leasing (Pipeline 33) into an ongoing connection service across the whole building network, not just directory-listed rooms: any building offering retail shop space, event venues, or conference/meeting areas earns KICC a commission whenever a business or event organizer is connected to that space through the platform, whether it's a one-off event booking or a longer-term shop lease. Revenue: booking commission for short-term/event use, leasing commission for longer-term shop or office space — the same building can generate both, at different times, from different tenants.

**81. Hotel-partner bundled conference & leisure experience**
A named extension of MICE tourism (Pipeline 40): a corporate client books a conference at a partner hotel (e.g., a Sarova property) and the platform offers, as an optional add-on at booking time, the full experience around it — accommodation at the venue, a white-sand-beach or equivalent leisure component, dining, and an additional destination facilitated by the hotel partner for the same trip. Every add-on is optional and itemized in the itinerary, but bundling removes the planning burden that would otherwise make a guest decline extras — the value proposition is exactly that it's easier to accept than to plan separately. Revenue: the same blended commission stack as bundled Tourism (flight/transfer/accommodation/experience commissions plus a curated-package markup), applied specifically to corporate/conference travel rather than leisure travel, which is a higher-value, less price-sensitive booking than a typical leisure itinerary.

**82. Airline & travel-partner MOU network**
Formal MOUs with airlines and other travel partners extend the bundled-itinerary model (Pipelines 37–43, 81) to cover flights directly, including on short notice — enabling a full urgent-meeting travel arrangement (flight plus ground transport plus accommodation) to be booked as one itinerary rather than assembled piecemeal. This is infrastructure rather than a separate customer-facing product: it's what makes the "book a conference, get the whole trip" experience in Pipeline 81 work for time-sensitive business travel, not just planned leisure trips. Revenue: affiliate/commission arrangement with each airline partner under the MOU, captured as part of the same bundled-itinerary commission stack rather than billed separately.

## M. Flagship Venues, Universal Site Ticketing, Investment, Multi-Zone SEZ, and Bulk Trade

**83. Flagship venue & stadium booking (Kasarani, Nyayo, Sarit Expo Centre, KICC itself, Bomas of Kenya, indoor arenas)**
Distinct from the generic building-space connection model (Pipeline 80) because these are large-scale, often government- or parastatal-owned venues — national stadiums, major indoor arenas, purpose-built expo centres, cultural venues like Bomas of Kenya, and KICC's own physical facility — with booking dynamics that don't resemble a church hall or office boardroom: concerts, national ceremonies, large-scale expos, and international conferences, often booked months in advance with complex logistics (security, crowd management, broadcast rights) layered on top of the venue hire itself. Revenue: a facilitation/booking commission on the venue-hire value, higher per-transaction than Pipeline 80 given the scale, plus a potential ancillary-services referral fee (security, catering, AV at this scale is usually a separate high-value contract) booked through the same platform.

**84. Universal site & attraction visit ticketing**
Generalizes ticketing beyond the specific tourism categories already named (wildlife/safari, cultural/heritage) to cover any site visit paid through the platform: a factory tour arranged for an SEZ investor doing due diligence, a government facility visit, a hotel-arranged excursion, or any attraction not otherwise captured by Pipelines 37–43. If a visit to a site is booked and paid through KICC, KICC earns a ticketing commission on it — the point of naming this separately is that the commission mechanism should not be limited to the named tourism sub-categories; any site, anywhere, any purpose of visit, is in scope. Revenue: ticketing commission (typically 10–20%, consistent with the existing attraction-ticketing rate), extended to cover business and institutional site visits, not just leisure tourism.

**85. Equity & investment facilitation**
Distinct from lending (Pipeline 62) and credit-data (Pipelines 60–61): this pipeline is about KICC — or third-party investors matched through KICC — taking an equity stake or direct investment position in businesses operating on the platform, whether a growing exhibitor, an agriculture aggregator, or a manufacturer in the Industrial/SEZ pipeline. Two mechanisms, and they can run independently: (a) KICC operates its own investment arm, taking minority equity stakes in high-performing platform businesses identified through their Trust Score and transaction history — the same verified data already built for Credit-Data-as-a-Service becomes underwriting data for equity decisions too; (b) KICC matches platform businesses seeking capital to third-party investors (angel networks, diaspora investment clubs from Pipeline 43, private equity) for a facilitation/success fee on the capital raised, without KICC's own balance sheet being involved. Revenue: (a) dividends and equity appreciation over time — a long-horizon, higher-risk line, and one that likely needs its own regulatory review (holding equity stakes may trigger different licensing than a marketplace or lender); (b) a facilitation fee (typically 1–3% of capital raised, standard for this kind of introduction) with no licensing complexity, since KICC never takes a position itself.

**86. Multi-zone SEZ marketplace — solving the "which zone" problem**
Kenya has multiple Special Economic Zones and designated industrial areas across different counties, and the reason this is hard to monetize as a single pipeline is that each zone has a different operator, different available plots, and different investor fit — treating "SEZ investment" as one line (as Pipelines 22–23 originally did) hides that an investor's real decision is which zone, not whether to invest in a zone at all. The fix is the same pattern already built for real estate at the building level (the Discovery & Directory layer, Pipeline 51): treat each SEZ as inventory, not as a one-off relationship. Concretely: (1) each zone authority or private zone operator onboards once, the same cascaded-onboarding logic used for landlords, and lists its available plots, existing tenant mix, and infrastructure readiness; (2) KICC becomes the single cross-zone marketplace an investor searches once, filtering by sector, workforce availability, logistics access, and incentive terms, instead of approaching each zone separately; (3) the matchmaking/recommender engine already specified for Pipeline 23 now runs across all listed zones simultaneously, and KICC earns its success fee (0.1–0.5% of committed investment) regardless of which zone the investor ultimately chooses — making KICC zone-agnostic and structurally neutral, which is exactly what makes it useful to an investor comparing options, and something no single zone authority could credibly offer on its own. Two additional revenue layers this structure unlocks: a zone-operator subscription fee for being listed and marketed to the investor pipeline (the same mechanism as exhibitor subscriptions, Pipeline 45, applied to zone operators instead of individual businesses), and a paid zone-comparison/advisory report for investors evaluating multiple zones side by side — a data product built entirely from information the marketplace already holds. This is the same building-level inventory insight applied one level up: buildings hold tenants, zones hold plots, and the same directory-plus-matchmaking pattern solves both.

**87. Bulk trade brokerage — corporate-to-institution wholesale matching**
Distinct from every smallholder-aggregation pipeline in Section A: here the seller is already a large, established corporation with bulk supply (a manufacturer, a large-scale processor, a bulk commodity trader) rather than a fragmented producer base needing aggregation. KICC's role is market access and negotiation facilitation at scale — onboarding these corporations and actively marketing their bulk supply to other institutions that need it, locally and internationally: a hospital network needing bulk medical supplies, a hotel chain needing bulk food-service inputs, an international buyer needing a large single-source order that the corporation can fulfil but doesn't have the institutional sales relationships to find on its own. Revenue: commission on matched bulk-trade value (typically thinner percentage than smallholder aggregation given the much larger transaction size — 1–3%), with the volume making up for the lower rate. This also functions as a natural export channel for several Section A and Section B commodities once they've been processed or consolidated by a corporate buyer, and as a demand-side complement to the supply-side aggregation pipelines already in this catalog.

## Note on how to read this list

Several entries above share a revenue *mechanism* (export commission, matchmaking success fee, embedded distribution) but are listed separately because each has a genuinely different buyer, price cycle, aggregation problem, or regulatory touchpoint — coffee and gold are both "commodity aggregation," but one clears through an existing auction system and the other is addressing active informal-market exploitation, and treating them as one pipeline would hide that difference. Where a pipeline explicitly reuses another's mechanism with no new take rate (Diaspora Gateway, Title Insurance, several tourism sub-types), that's stated directly rather than implying it's incremental revenue on top of what's already counted elsewhere in a consolidated model.

This catalog is deliberately not re-run through the phased revenue/sequencing model from the prior document — that reconciliation (which of these ship first, which are blocked, and the honest ceiling against the $12M/month target) still holds and applies line-by-line to everything above; nothing here should be read as changing those numbers, only as making visible every individual line that feeds into them.
